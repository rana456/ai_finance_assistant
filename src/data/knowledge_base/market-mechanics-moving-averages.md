# Moving Averages and Trends

A moving average smooths out the day-to-day noise in a stock's price to make the underlying direction easier to see. It's one of the most common ways to describe a trend.

## What a moving average is

A 50-day moving average is simply the average closing price over the last 50 trading days, recalculated each day. Because it averages many days together, it moves more slowly and smoothly than the daily price. The 50-day average reflects the medium-term trend; the 200-day average reflects the long-term trend.

## Reading the trend

When the current price is above its moving average, the stock has generally been rising; when it's below, it has generally been falling. Comparing the two averages adds context: when the 50-day average is above the 200-day average, the medium-term trend is stronger than the long-term one, often described as an uptrend. The reverse — 50-day below 200-day — is often described as a downtrend. When they're close together and the price is drifting, the stock is roughly sideways.

## An important caution

Moving averages describe the *past*. They summarize where a price has been, not where it's going. A stock in an uptrend can reverse, and one in a downtrend can recover. Trends are context, not predictions — they help you understand a stock's recent behavior, not forecast its future.

## Key takeaways

- A moving average is the average price over a window (e.g. 50 or 200 days), smoothing out daily noise.
- Price above its average and 50-day above 200-day generally indicate an uptrend; the opposite indicates a downtrend.
- Moving averages summarize the past; they do not predict future prices.
