# The Three Tax Treatments

The single most useful idea in investment taxes is that almost every account falls into one of three tax treatments. Once you see which bucket an account is in, its rules make sense. Note that specific contribution limits and income rules change every year — always verify current figures at IRS.gov.

## 1. Tax-deferred (pay taxes later)

With a tax-deferred account, you often deduct contributions from your income today, so you pay less tax now. Your money then grows without being taxed year to year, and you pay ordinary income tax when you withdraw it later — typically in retirement. Traditional 401(k)s and Traditional IRAs work this way. The idea: get a tax break now, pay tax on the way out.

## 2. Tax-free / Roth (pay taxes now)

With a Roth account, you contribute money you've already paid tax on, so there's no deduction today. In exchange, your money grows and — if you follow the rules — comes out completely tax-free in retirement, including all the investment growth. Roth 401(k)s and Roth IRAs work this way. The idea: pay tax now, owe nothing later.

## 3. Taxable (pay as you go)

A regular taxable brokerage account gets no special tax treatment. There are no contribution limits and no withdrawal penalties, but you pay tax along the way — on dividends and interest each year, and on capital gains when you sell an investment for a profit.

## Why it matters: tax diversification

Because no one knows their future tax rate, many people spread money across more than one treatment — some tax-deferred, some Roth, some taxable. This "tax diversification" gives flexibility to manage taxes in retirement.

## Key takeaways

- Tax-deferred: deduct now, pay ordinary tax on withdrawal (Traditional 401(k)/IRA).
- Roth/tax-free: no deduction now, tax-free qualified withdrawals (Roth 401(k)/IRA).
- Taxable: no special treatment, taxed as you go (brokerage accounts).
- Specific limits and rules change yearly — verify at IRS.gov.
