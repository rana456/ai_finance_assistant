# 529 Education Savings Plans

A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged account designed to save for education. It's sponsored by states, though you can generally invest in many states' plans regardless of where you live. Rules and qualified-expense definitions change over time — verify current details at IRS.gov.

## How the tax benefit works

Money in a 529 grows without being taxed year to year, and withdrawals are tax-free when used for qualified education expenses — such as college tuition, fees, room and board, and, within limits, some K-12 tuition. The federal government doesn't offer a deduction for contributions, but many states offer their own state-tax deduction or credit for residents who contribute.

## Qualified vs. non-qualified withdrawals

The tax-free benefit applies only to qualified education expenses. If you withdraw money for something else, the earnings portion is generally taxed as income and may face an additional penalty. The contributions you put in (which were already taxed) aren't penalized — only the growth.

## Flexibility features

529 plans have grown more flexible over time. Features can include changing the beneficiary to another family member, and — subject to conditions and limits — options to repurpose unused funds. Because these provisions change, confirm what's currently allowed at IRS.gov.

## Key takeaways

- A 529 plan offers tax-free growth and withdrawals for qualified education expenses.
- No federal deduction, but many states offer one for residents.
- Non-qualified withdrawals are taxed and may be penalized on the earnings; rules change — verify at IRS.gov.
