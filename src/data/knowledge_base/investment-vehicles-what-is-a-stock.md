# What Is a Stock?

A stock (also called a share or equity) is a piece of ownership in a company. When you buy one share of a company, you own a small fraction of that business and become a shareholder. If the company issues one million shares and you own one, you own one-millionth of the company.

## Why companies issue stock

Companies sell stock to raise money. Instead of borrowing from a bank, a company can sell ownership stakes to the public and use that cash to grow — hire people, build products, or expand. In exchange, shareholders get a claim on the company's future success.

## How investors make (or lose) money

There are two main ways a stock can put money in your pocket:

- **Price appreciation.** If the company becomes more valuable, the price of each share can rise, and you can sell for more than you paid. If the company struggles, the price can fall, and you can lose money.
- **Dividends.** Some companies share a portion of their profits with shareholders as regular cash payments called dividends. Not all companies pay them.

There is no guarantee of either. A stock's price can go up or down for many reasons, and a company can reduce or stop paying dividends at any time.

## The risk of owning individual stocks

Owning a single company's stock ties your money to that one company's fate. If that business does poorly, your investment can lose significant value — even go to zero if the company fails. This company-specific risk is one reason many beginners choose diversified funds, which spread money across many companies, rather than betting on a single stock.

## Common vs. preferred stock

Most everyday investors buy **common stock**, which usually comes with voting rights at shareholder meetings and the potential for dividends. **Preferred stock** typically has no voting rights but gets priority for dividend payments. Beginners mostly encounter common stock.

## Key takeaways

- A stock is partial ownership of a company.
- You can earn money through price appreciation and dividends, but neither is guaranteed.
- Individual stocks carry company-specific risk; diversification helps manage it.
