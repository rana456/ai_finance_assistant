# Traditional vs. Roth

"Traditional" and "Roth" describe *when* you pay tax on retirement savings. It's the same choice whether you're looking at a 401(k) or an IRA. Contribution limits and income eligibility rules change each year — check IRS.gov for current figures.

## Traditional: a tax break now

Traditional contributions are usually made with pre-tax money — you may deduct them from your taxable income today, lowering this year's tax bill. The money grows tax-deferred, and you pay ordinary income tax on withdrawals in retirement. Traditional accounts often appeal to people who expect to be in a lower tax bracket later than they are now.

## Roth: a tax break later

Roth contributions are made with after-tax money — no deduction today. In return, qualified withdrawals in retirement, including all the growth, are completely tax-free. Roth accounts often appeal to people who expect to be in the same or a higher tax bracket later, or who simply value the certainty of tax-free income in retirement.

## The core trade-off

The decision hinges on a comparison you can't make with certainty: your tax rate now versus in retirement.

- If your rate will be **lower** in retirement, Traditional's up-front deduction tends to win.
- If your rate will be **higher** (or you want certainty), Roth's tax-free withdrawals tend to win.

Because the future is unknown, many people hold both to hedge.

## Other differences to know

Roth IRAs have income limits that can restrict who may contribute directly, and the two types differ on required withdrawals later in life. These rules change, so confirm the current specifics at IRS.gov or with a tax professional.

## Key takeaways

- Traditional = deduct now, pay tax on withdrawal; Roth = no deduction now, tax-free qualified withdrawals.
- The choice depends on your tax rate now vs. later — unknowable, so many hold both.
- Income limits and withdrawal rules apply and change yearly; verify at IRS.gov.
