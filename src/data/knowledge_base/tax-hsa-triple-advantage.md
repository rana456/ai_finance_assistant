# Health Savings Accounts (HSA)

A Health Savings Account (HSA) is a tax-advantaged account for medical expenses, available to people enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible health plan. It's notable for a rare "triple tax advantage." Eligibility rules and annual contribution limits are set by the IRS and change yearly — check IRS.gov.

## The triple tax advantage

An HSA gets favorable tax treatment at all three stages, which is unusual:

1. **Going in** — contributions are tax-deductible (or pre-tax through payroll), lowering taxable income.
2. **Growing** — the money grows without being taxed year to year.
3. **Coming out** — withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free.

Most accounts give you a break at only one or two of these stages; the HSA offers all three for medical spending.

## Why some treat it as a retirement account

HSA funds roll over year to year — they aren't "use it or lose it" — and the account stays with you even if you change jobs or health plans. Because of the triple advantage, some people pay current medical costs out of pocket and let the HSA grow invested for later, treating it as a supplemental retirement account for future medical expenses. After a certain age, non-medical withdrawals are treated more like a Traditional retirement account (taxed as income rather than penalized).

## Important conditions

The triple tax benefit applies to *qualified* medical expenses; non-qualified withdrawals can be taxed and penalized. You must be enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible plan to contribute. These rules change and depend on your situation — verify at IRS.gov.

## Key takeaways

- An HSA offers a triple tax advantage: deductible in, tax-free growth, tax-free out for medical costs.
- Requires a qualifying high-deductible health plan; funds roll over and stay with you.
- Some use it as a long-term investment account for future medical expenses. Verify eligibility and limits at IRS.gov.
