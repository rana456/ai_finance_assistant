# Reading a Stock Quote

When you look up a stock, you see a handful of numbers. Here's what the common ones mean and what they signal.

## Price and daily change

The price is the most recent amount a share traded for. The daily change — shown as a dollar amount and a percentage — compares it to the previous day's closing price. A green +1.5% means the stock is up one and a half percent since yesterday's close; red means it's down.

## Day's range

The day's range shows the lowest and highest prices the stock has traded at so far today. A wide range means the price is bouncing around a lot (more volatile today); a narrow range means it's relatively steady.

## 52-week range

The 52-week range is the lowest and highest price over the past year. It gives context for today's price: a stock near its 52-week high has climbed a lot recently, while one near its 52-week low has fallen. It doesn't tell you whether the stock is "cheap" or "expensive" in value terms — just where it sits versus its own recent history.

## Volume

Volume is how many shares changed hands. Comparing today's volume to the average daily volume tells you whether trading is unusually busy. High volume often accompanies big news, because more people are reacting to it.

## P/E ratio

The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio divides the share price by the company's earnings per share. It roughly answers "how much am I paying for each dollar of the company's annual profit?" A P/E of 20 means investors pay $20 per $1 of yearly earnings. A high P/E often signals that investors expect strong future growth; a low P/E can signal modest expectations or an out-of-favor company. P/E is most meaningful when compared to similar companies, not in isolation.

## Key takeaways

- Price and daily change show the latest value and how it moved since yesterday.
- Day's range and 52-week range give short- and long-term context.
- Volume signals how active trading is; the P/E ratio signals how richly a stock is priced relative to its earnings.
