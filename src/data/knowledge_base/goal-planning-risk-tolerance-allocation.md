# Risk Tolerance and Asset Allocation

Asset allocation — how you split money between stocks, bonds, and cash — is one of the biggest drivers of both your expected return and how much your portfolio bounces around. Two factors shape a sensible allocation: your risk tolerance and your time horizon.

## What risk tolerance means

Risk tolerance is how much short-term ups and downs you can handle, both financially and emotionally. Someone who would panic and sell during a 20% drop has a lower tolerance than someone who would calmly hold or buy more. It's personal — there's no "right" answer — and being honest about it matters, because a plan you abandon in a downturn is worse than a steadier one you can stick with.

## Common allocation frameworks

Financial educators often describe three broad, illustrative frameworks:

- **Conservative** — roughly 25% stocks, 70% bonds, 5% cash. Prioritizes stability; lower expected return, smaller swings.
- **Moderate** — roughly 60% stocks, 40% bonds. A balanced middle that captures growth while bonds cushion the falls.
- **Aggressive** — roughly 85% stocks, 15% bonds. Prioritizes long-term growth; higher expected return, bigger swings.

These are general starting points for education, not personal recommendations.

## Why time horizon matters

Time horizon — how long until you need the money — can matter as much as tolerance. A longer horizon can absorb more volatility, because there's time to recover from downturns before you need to spend the money. A short horizon argues for a more conservative mix even for someone with a high risk tolerance, because a badly-timed drop right before the goal leaves no time to bounce back. This is the idea behind target-date funds, which automatically grow more conservative as the goal approaches.

## Key takeaways

- Asset allocation is a major driver of both expected return and volatility.
- Conservative, moderate, and aggressive are common illustrative frameworks (roughly 25/60/85% stocks).
- A longer time horizon can justify more stocks; a short horizon argues for a more conservative mix regardless of tolerance.
